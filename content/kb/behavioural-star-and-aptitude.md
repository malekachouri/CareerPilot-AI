# The STAR method

- **Situation**: one or two sentences of context: team, project, constraint.
- **Task**: your responsibility or goal.
- **Action**: what *you* did, step by step. Say "I", not only "we"; include the reasoning behind choices.
- **Result**: the outcome, measurable when possible (time saved, incidents reduced), and what you learned.

Keep answers to about two minutes. Prepare 6-8 real stories (a conflict, a failure, a success you led, a
tight deadline, learning something fast, disagreeing with a decision) and adapt them to each question.
Be honest: interviewers dig into details with follow-up questions.

# Aptitude practice methods

- **Numerical**: convert everything to the same unit first; percentages compound (a 20% rise then a 20% fall
  is ×1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96); estimate before calculating to spot impossible options.
- **Verbal (true / false / cannot be determined)**: judge only from the text. "Cannot be determined" is correct
  when the passage neither confirms nor contradicts the statement, even if it sounds plausible.
- **Logical**: write the constraints down and test each option against all of them; eliminate quickly.
- **Abstract**: look for one change at a time: count, rotation, position, colour, number of sides.
- **Personality questionnaires** are about preferences, not right answers: answer consistently and honestly.
  Practice versions are for self-reflection only, not clinical or validated assessments.
